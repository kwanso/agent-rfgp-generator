# Questions for the client

A few questions that help us set up the system correctly from day one.

### 1. Applicant details in your initial form

Please share the details you ask the foreign national for in your initial form, for example name, email, date of birth, address and passport details. If you already use an intake form or questionnaire, a blank copy is perfect.

**Why we ask:** The applicant screen is built from this list, and these details are what the system uses to fill in the DS-160 and other forms.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Start with the basic details (name, email, phone, date of birth, nationality, address and passport details) and add the rest of the DS-160 questions step by step.

### 2. Forms you want the system to fill in

Please share the forms you want the system to prefill, for example the DS-160, DS-156E, G-28 or I-129. A blank copy of each form is ideal, and one filled example (with personal details removed) helps even more.

**Why we ask:** Each form is set up once in the system, with every box linked to the right applicant detail, so the system can fill it in automatically.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Start with the DS-160, and add other forms once you send them. Your team can also upload new blank forms later themselves.

### 3. Documents you ask foreign nationals for

Please share the list of documents you ask foreign nationals to provide, for example passport, bank statements, business plan, source of funds proof, lease and company registration.

**Why we ask:** Each document type gets its own place in the system, and for each one we decide which details the system should read from it.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Start with passport, bank statement, business plan and "other", and add more document types as you share them.

### 4. Your E2 checklist

Could you share the E2 checklist you use in the consultation with the attorney and paralegal? For each item, is a simple yes or no enough, or does the team also need to write notes?

**Why we ask:** The consultation screen is built from your checklist, so knowing each item lets us show the right kind of answer for it.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Start with a short sample checklist where every item has a Yes / No / Unsure answer and a space for notes, then swap in your list as soon as you send it. Consultations already done keep the checklist they were done with.

### 5. Extra-protected personal details

Which personal details should be extra-protected, meaning hidden on screen until a staff member clicks "show", with a record of who looked? For example passport numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, national ID or tax numbers, bank account numbers, and the DS-160 security and criminal-history answers. Which of these do your staff need to search by?

**Why we ask:** These details are locked away from the first day they are entered, and changing the list later means reprocessing every record already saved.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Hide passport numbers, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, A-numbers, national ID and tax numbers, bank account numbers and the DS-160 security answers until someone clicks "show". Everything read from documents or typed into forms is also stored securely. Staff can still find an applicant by name, email, date of birth or passport number.

### 6. Keeping and deleting data

How long must you keep an applicant's documents and personal details after their case is closed? If a former applicant asks you to delete their data (for example under European privacy law), do you need to be able to do that, and is there anything you must keep for legal reasons?

**Why we ask:** Deletion rules are much easier to build in before real applicant data arrives than to add afterwards.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Keep everything for 7 years after a case is closed. Anything staff delete is hidden rather than destroyed, so it can be recovered. If someone asks for their data to be deleted, an administrator removes their personal details and documents by hand.

### 7. Families applying together

When an investor's spouse and children apply at the same time, do you treat the family as one case, or does each person have a separate case? Does each family member need their own DS-160 and their own documents?

**Why we ask:** This decides whether a family's details, documents and forms are kept together in one place or spread across separate files.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Treat the family as one case led by the investor, with each person keeping their own details, documents and DS-160. The first version records the investor only, and family members can be added to the same case later without changing anything already entered.

### 8. Coming back for another case

Can the same person come to you for more than one case over the years, for example an E-2 renewal and later a green card? Should each case keep its own documents and forms? And do you want to keep people who enquire but never become clients?

**Why we ask:** This decides whether a person's details are entered once and reused for every later case, or whether each person simply has one file.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Give each case its own documents and forms, while the person's details are entered once and reused. The first case is created automatically when you add a new applicant, so day to day your team simply works with the applicant. Someone who enquires but does not go ahead stays on file, with their case marked as stopped or closed.

### 9. Information read from documents

When the system reads details from an uploaded document, such as the passport number or expiry date, should it update the applicant's record by itself, or should a staff member always approve it first? When two documents disagree, which one should win?

**Why we ask:** This decides whether an applicant's record can change without a person checking it first.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Have a staff member approve every value before it changes the applicant's record. Forms can still be filled in with a value that has not been approved yet, but it is marked "needs review". When documents disagree, the passport wins for identity details, otherwise the more specific document wins, and staff are shown the difference.

### 10. Who can see what

Should every member of your team see every applicant, or only the cases they work on? Later on, will anyone outside your team, such as applicants themselves or attorneys from another firm, need their own login, for example to fill in their details or upload documents?

**Why we ask:** This decides how we set up access so that each person sees only what they should, now and as more people join.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Let every staff member see every applicant, with no outside logins in the first version. Limiting staff to their own cases, and giving applicants their own login, can be added later.

### 11. Adding your own questions

Will your team want to add or change the questions asked about applicants themselves, or is it fine for Kwanso to make those changes in a regular update?

**Why we ask:** This decides whether the list of questions is something your team edits in the system or something Kwanso maintains for you.

**If we don't hear back, we will:** Have Kwanso maintain the applicant questions and the DS-160 questions in regular updates. Your team can upload new blank forms and match each box on them to the applicant's details yourselves.
